# Laboratory Reports

The reports are ordered from the latest and most advanced work to the earlier foundational labs.

## 01 - Lab 13 - Digital Phase Modulation and QAM

Explored digital modulation from ASK, FSK, and BPSK through more advanced phase and quadrature methods. The lab examined IQ mapping and constellation diagrams for QPSK, higher-order PSK, and QAM formats including 4-QAM, 8-QAM, 16-QAM, 32-QAM, and 64-QAM, with emphasis on bits per symbol, data rate, phase and amplitude states, and the effect of noise on constellation-point separation.

## 02 - Lab 12 - Pulse Amplitude Modulation and Sampling

Created and analyzed pulse-amplitude-modulated signals in both the time and frequency domains. The lab applied the Nyquist sampling theorem, investigated proper sampling and aliasing, observed repeated spectral components and sidebands with a spectrum analyzer, and examined low-pass reconstruction of sampled signals.

## 03 - Lab 11 - Pulse Width Modulation

Investigated pulse-width modulation by relating duty cycle to average voltage and observing how analog information can be represented by changing pulse width. The work included sinusoidal PWM, oscilloscope measurements, pulse-width variation, and low-pass-filter recovery of an analog waveform, with connections to motor and servo control.

## 04 - Lab 10 - FM Demodulation and PLL

Studied FM demodulation using both a slope detector and a CD4046 phase-locked loop. The lab converted frequency changes back into voltage, examined limiter behavior, pseudo-AM generation and diode detection, PLL lock and tracking, VCO rest frequency, and recovery of sine, square, and triangle modulation signals.

## 05 - Lab 9 - Frequency Modulation

Analyzed frequency modulation in the time and frequency domains. The lab related carrier frequency, modulation frequency, frequency deviation, and modulation index, used Bessel-function behavior to interpret FM sidebands, and measured how increasing modulation index redistributes signal energy and changes occupied bandwidth.

## 06 - Lab 8 - RF Mixers and Frequency Conversion

Investigated RF mixer operation and heterodyning using local-oscillator and RF inputs. The work measured sum and difference products, conversion loss, unwanted intermodulation products, frequency translation to a fixed intermediate frequency, signal-level variation, and the practical need for filtering and automatic gain control in receiver systems.

## 07 - Lab 7 - Amplitude Modulation and Demodulation

Generated and measured amplitude-modulated signals while varying modulation index from unmodulated carrier conditions through overmodulation. The lab compared time-domain envelopes with frequency-domain carrier and sidebands, tested different message waveforms, and recovered the original information using a diode-resistor-capacitor envelope detector.

## 08 - Lab 6 - FFT Fourier Analysis and Filtering

Used oscilloscope FFT analysis to connect time-domain waveforms with their frequency-domain components. The lab examined sine, square, pulse, sinc, cardiac, and noise signals, duty-cycle-dependent harmonic nulls, sampling and Nyquist considerations, FFT windowing, and how low-pass filtering progressively removes harmonics and changes waveform shape.

## 09 - Lab 5C - Spectrum Analysis and Fourier Harmonics

Compared sine, square, triangle, and pulse waveforms using a spectrum analyzer and Fourier-series predictions. The lab examined harmonic spacing and amplitude, duty-cycle effects, sinc-shaped spectral envelopes, logarithmic versus linear display, sampling and aliasing, and the relationship between total RMS power and the sum of harmonic powers.

## 10 - Lab 5B - RMS dB and Signal Measurements

Measured and calculated peak-to-peak, average, and RMS values for sine, square, triangle, and pulse signals, including cases with DC offset. The work reinforced waveform-specific RMS relationships, duty-cycle effects, decibels and dBm, power calculations, and comparison of measured values with analytical and software-based results.

## 11 - Lab 5A - Spectrum Analyzer and Fourier Series

Introduced practical spectrum-analyzer operation and frequency-domain measurement. The lab examined a fundamental and its harmonics, 50-ohm loading, dBm power levels, marker measurements, resolution bandwidth, dynamic range, and the relationship between Fourier-series concepts and measured spectra.

## 12 - Lab 4 - Bubba Oscillator and Barkhausen Criterion

Built and analyzed a multi-stage RC phase-shift oscillator. The lab applied the Barkhausen gain and phase conditions, calculated oscillation frequency, measured phase shift and loop gain, adjusted amplifier gain for sustained oscillation, and examined how component values and feedback determine stable sine-wave generation.

## 13 - Lab 3 - Crystal Resonance and Frequency Response Analysis

Studied oscilloscope-probe loading and compensation, high-Q crystal resonance, and automated frequency-response measurement. The lab determined resonant frequency, -3 dB bandwidth, and Q factor for a crystal and used oscilloscope Frequency Response Analyzer mode to measure gain and phase for band-pass and low-pass filters.

## 14 - Labs 1-2 - Analog Filter Frequency Response

Developed the foundation for communication-system filtering through passive RC, RL, and RLC networks. The work examined low-pass, high-pass, band-pass, and band-stop behavior, cutoff and resonant frequency, phase response, transfer functions, bandwidth, and Q factor through calculation, simulation, and measurement.

## Tools and Methods

Spectrum analyzers, oscilloscopes, function and arbitrary-waveform generators, FFT and frequency-response analysis, RF mixers, filters, AM/FM/PM and digital modulation, PLLs, IQ constellations, sampling and Nyquist analysis, Fourier series, RMS and dB/dBm calculations, PCB soldering, and communication-system troubleshooting.
